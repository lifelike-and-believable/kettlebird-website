# Putting stories on the site

<!-- docs/user/publishing-stories.md. For Star (persona-artist): putting a story, one-shot or in
chapters, on the site with the editor. Written from design-story-reader, and design-story-import for uploading
Word documents. -->

Your stories can be read right on the site, in one comfortable column, on a phone or a computer. You put a story up by uploading it as a Word document, one document per chapter, pictures and all, and a long story can go up a chapter at a time, the way comic chapters do. Your Word documents stay where your writing lives: to change a word, change the document and upload it again.

Everything here happens in the editor, at `kettlebird.com/studio`, in **Writing**. **Publish** is at the bottom of the open item, and the status at the top right says when your change is live ([When your change is live](going-live.md)).

## Start a new story

1. In **Writing**, choose **New story**, and type the **Title**. The **Web address** fills itself in from the title: it's the end of the story's link, like `kettlebird.com/writing/the-kettles-song`. Once people have shared links, leave it alone.
2. Add a **Short description**, a **Cover** and **Genres** if you like.
3. If the story has sex scenes, graphic violence or other strong content, tick **Mature or explicit content**. Readers are asked before the text shows, and the Writing page says so. Left unticked, readers go straight to the text.
4. If readers should know something before they start, write it in **Content notes**, such as "Mild violence". It shows before the story.
5. Then either upload the whole story into **Text** (a one-shot, below), or add chapters (further below).
6. Choose **Publish**.

A story with **Mature or explicit content** ticked asks readers before showing its text: they see "This story has mature or explicit content.", your notes, and **Continue to the story**. Their browser remembers once they've continued, for every ticked story, so they aren't asked again on that browser. Its card on the Writing page and the top of its page say **Mature or explicit**. Unticked stories open straight to the text. The box covers the story and all its chapters.

## Upload the text

Save the chapter (or the one-shot) as a Word document, a `.docx`. From Google Docs: **File**, **Download**, **Microsoft Word (.docx)**. One document per chapter.

Before you upload:
- accept or reject any tracked changes (or Google Docs suggestions): they come in as if accepted;
- use Word's line spacing, not empty lines, to space the text out: two empty lines make a scene break;
- if a picture is cropped in Word, choose **Compress Pictures**, **Delete cropped areas**, or the whole picture comes in;
- the chapter's title as the first line is fine: it's left out, as the site shows the title anyway.

Then, in **Text**, choose **Upload a Word document** and pick the file, or drop it on the space below the button. While it's read you see "Reading The Long Road 3.docx…", then the chapter shows in **Text** as readers will get it, read-only.

What comes through:
- paragraphs, *italics* and **bold**, and line breaks within a paragraph (Shift+Enter, in poems and letters);
- scene breaks (below);
- your pictures, where they are in the text, each on its own line (below);
- headings, tables (each row as a line, its cells joined with " · ") and footnotes (at the end, numbered, with "[1]" where each was), as plain paragraphs.

Word's layout and picture sizes don't come through, nor text boxes, comments, or page headers and footers.

A note says what came in and what didn't, such as "Added The Long Road 3.docx: 48 paragraphs, 3 scene breaks and 3 pictures, with its italics and bold." and anything to check. It stays until you close it with ✕ or publish. Check the chapter, then publish.

### Change something

To change a word or a picture, change the document and upload it again: choose **Upload a new version**. The editor asks first, saying what's there now and what's coming ("Replace Chapter 3's text?"), so a wrong file stands out; **Cancel** changes nothing. Alt text and **Decorative** you set in the editor stay with pictures that are still there, unless you changed the alt text in Word.

Above the text, a line says which document is in and when it was added: "The Long Road 3.docx, added 2:38 pm, not published yet · Undo this upload". **Undo this upload** puts back what was there before, until you publish, even after you've left the page.

Keep your Word documents: they're where the writing lives.

## Pictures

Pictures in your document come in where they are. A picture beside the text, or in the middle of a line, moves to just after its paragraph, and the note says so. JPEG, PNG, GIF and WebP pictures come in; Word's own drawings and charts, and pictures in text boxes or tables, don't. Keep to a few pictures a chapter: many readers are on phones.

On the site, a picture is the width of the text column, or its own width if that's smaller, and readers can tap it to see it full screen.

Under **Text**, **Pictures** lists each one, with:
- **Alt text**: a few words saying what the picture shows, for people using screen readers. It's filled in from Word if the picture has alt text there (right-click it, **Edit Alt Text** or **View Alt Text**), marked "alt text from Word": check it. Word's automatic alt text ("… Description automatically generated") doesn't count, so write your own.
- **Decorative**: tick it for a picture that only decorates, like a divider or a flourish, so screen readers skip it. A picture marked decorative in Word is ticked already.

Until a picture has alt text or is decorative, it's marked "Alt text", and **Pictures** says "1 needs alt text". You can still publish; screen readers then just say "Picture".

Your pictures are uploaded when the document is read, and are public on the site once you publish. Pictures you replace or undo are removed when you next publish.

## Scene breaks

A scene break shows on the site as a small centred ⁂, with space around it. In the editor it shows as a "Scene break" bar. Your document makes one with:

- a line with only symbols on it, like `* * *`, `***`, `---`, `~` or `#`;
- a horizontal line in Word (**Insert**, **Horizontal Line**, or a line drawn under an empty paragraph) or Google Docs;
- a gap of two or more empty lines.

If the note says "That's a lot of scene breaks", your document probably uses empty lines for spacing: use line spacing instead, and upload it again.

To remove a scene break, delete it in the document and upload it again.

## Add a chapter to a story

For a story in chapters:

1. Open the story and choose **Add chapter**, under **Chapters**. The chapter opens beside the story, with the next number filled in.
2. Type a **Title** if the chapter has one, like "Over the Ridge". Without one, it's shown as "Chapter 3".
3. Upload the chapter's Word document into **Text** ([Upload the text](#upload-the-text)).
4. Choose **Publish**. A new story is published along with its first chapter.

Chapters are listed under the story in number order, each marked **On the site** or **Not published yet**. Readers only see published chapters.

On the story, set **Status** to **In progress** while you're still posting chapters, and **Complete** when the last one is out. Readers see it on the Writing page, on the story's page, and at the end of the newest chapter ("More to come." or "The end.").

If you started a story as a one-shot and want to split it, choose **Add chapter** on it: Chapter 1 opens with your story's text already in it, and the editor says "Your story's text is now in Chapter 1." Nothing is lost. Then upload each chapter's own document: Chapter 1's into Chapter 1 (**Upload a new version** replaces the whole story with it), and **Add chapter** for Chapter 2 and the rest. Publish Chapter 1 (and the others); from then on, the site shows the story's chapters.

To make a story in chapters a one-shot again, remove its chapters, then upload the whole story into the story's **Text**.

## Mature content and content notes

<!-- The rule of thumb below is a starting point, close to AO3's; Star to confirm or rewrite it. -->

Tick **Mature or explicit content** for what would be Mature or Explicit on AO3: sex scenes, graphic violence, strong themes. Readers are then asked before the text shows. Search engines are told too, so the story can be left out for people searching with safe search on.

Use **Content notes** for anything else a reader may want to know first, ticked or not: some violence, swearing, a scary scene. They show exactly as you write them, before the story. Keep them short: a list of what to expect works best. With no tick and no notes, nothing shows before the story.

## Check it, then publish

Choose **Preview** at the top of a story or a chapter to see its page before you publish ([See it before you publish](previewing.md)). The preview shows the text, ticked or not, with the line readers will see.

After **Publish**, **View on site** opens the story's page, or the chapter's.

## Links elsewhere

**Link to read it** is optional. For a story with its text on the site, readers see it as "Also on AO3" (or Ko-fi, Tumblr, Wattpad). While AO3 is offline, you may want to remove AO3 links, so readers don't follow a link that doesn't work.

A story with no text on the site works as before: its **Read it** button on the Writing page opens the link.

## If links will change

Changing the **Web address** of a published story, or the **Chapter** number of a published chapter, breaks links people have shared. The editor warns you before you publish, as it does for comics.

## Remove a story

Choose ⋯ next to **Publish**, then **Delete**. For a story in chapters, the editor asks once, naming the chapters: "Delete The Long Road and its 3 chapters?" Choose **Delete** to go ahead, or Cancel. The story and its chapters come off the site with the next update, and links people have shared stop working. This can't be undone.

## If something goes wrong

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Add a web address so readers can find this story." | A story with text or chapters needs its web address. | Fill in **Web address**, such as the title in small letters with hyphens: `the-kettles-song`. |
| A story with strong content opens straight to the text | **Mature or explicit content** wasn't ticked. | Tick it and publish again: the tag and the question show from the next update. |
| "Chapter 2 already exists in this story. Choose another number." | Two chapters have the same number. | Change the number on one of them. |
| "Add the chapter's text." | The chapter is empty. | Upload its Word document. |
| "This story has chapters, so its text is in them. To make it a one-shot again, remove its chapters and upload the whole story here." | The story has chapters, so its own **Text** isn't used. | Upload to the chapters. To go back to a one-shot, remove the chapters, then upload the whole story into the story's **Text**. |
| "Notes.pdf isn't a Word document." | Only `.docx` files can be uploaded. | In Word, **File**, **Save As**, **Word Document**; in Google Docs, **File**, **Download**, **Microsoft Word (.docx)**. |
| "… is an older Word file, which can't be read here." | A `.doc` from an older Word. | Open it in Word or Google Docs, save or download it as `.docx`, and upload that. |
| "… is a link to a Google Doc, not the document." | A `.gdoc` is a shortcut in Google Drive. | In Google Docs, **File**, **Download**, **Microsoft Word (.docx)**, and upload that. |
| "… is locked with a password." | The document has a password. | Save an unlocked copy and upload that. |
| "… is over 50 MB, too big to read here." | Its pictures are very large. | In Word, **Compress Pictures**, or split the chapter. |
| "Picture 2 … couldn't be added" | A Word drawing, or a picture in a format the site can't show. | Save it in the document as a picture (JPEG or PNG) and upload again, or publish without it. |
| Something you deleted in Word is still there | Tracked changes came in as if accepted. | Accept or reject the changes in Word, and upload it again. |
| A scene break shows as `* * *` text | It was in the middle of other text. | Put it on its own line in the document, and upload it again. |
